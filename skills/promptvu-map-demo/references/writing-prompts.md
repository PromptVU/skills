# Writing prompts that sell

A prompt is what the rep will say at that moment on the call. Write for the ear, not the page.

## The rules

- Write in the rep's own voice. Match their tone and personality, not a generic sales voice.
- One or two sentences. If the rep can't glance at it and say it, it's too long.
- Be solution focused. Connect what's on screen to the result this buyer cares about: time saved, revenue gained, costs cut or risk reduced.
- Follow the order: the buyer's problem, what this screen does about it, the result.
- One specific per prompt: a number, an outcome or a question to ask the buyer.
- One prompt per moment, on the element the rep will point at.
- Only use facts from the rep's knowledge base or what the buyer actually said. Never invent prices, customers, results or features. If you need a fact, ask.
- Use the buyer's own words when you have them. "You said onboarding takes six weeks" beats "Onboarding can be slow."
- Don't feature dump. If a screen doesn't support this buyer's priorities, say so in your report and suggest skipping it.

## Weak vs strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "This is our pricing page. We have three plans." | "You said reps lose six hours a week to manual reporting. Business gives that back, which covers the cost in the first quarter." |
| "Here's the dashboard with lots of great metrics." | "This is the one number your VP asked about on our last call: pipeline created per rep. Watch it update live." |
| "We integrate with many tools." | "You're on Salesforce, so this syncs both ways. No one re-enters a deal." |

## Questions are prompts too

Some of the best prompts are a question the rep asks at that moment:

- "Before I click into this, how are you handling approvals today?"
- "Is this the report your CFO would want, or would she cut it differently?"

Use a question when the rep needs the buyer to confirm pain before showing the answer.
